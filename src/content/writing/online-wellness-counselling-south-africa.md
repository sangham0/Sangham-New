Online counselling has become ordinary enough that the practical question is no longer whether a meaningful conversation can happen by video. The useful questions are what kind of counselling is being offered, who it is for, what it costs, and what happens when the situation requires a different level of care.

Sangham offers online wellness counselling to people across South Africa and internationally. I am South African-registered, currently based in India, and work online in English.

This page explains the service plainly.

For a direct comparison with statutory mental health professions, read [Wellness Counsellor vs Psychologist in South Africa](/wisdom/wellness-counsellor-vs-psychologist-south-africa).

## What Wellness Counselling Means

Wellness counselling is supportive, developmental work. It can be appropriate for life transitions, recurring personal or relationship patterns, stress that is not at crisis level, questions of identity or direction, and the difficulty of translating insight into changed behaviour.

The work may include reflective conversation, breath and regulation practices, attention training, body-based awareness, and practical experiments between sessions. The approach is integrative, but the scope remains clear.

Wellness counselling is not clinical psychology, psychiatry, psychotherapy, diagnosis, psychometric assessment, or emergency mental health care. I am registered with the Association for Supportive Counsellors and Holistic Practitioners under ASCHP registration number 10559.

If what you describe requires clinical assessment or treatment, I will say so and recommend seeking an appropriate psychologist, GP, psychiatrist, or emergency service.

## Who It May Suit

People often reach out because they understand a pattern but remain caught in it. They may be dealing with:

- overthinking, anxiety, or persistent internal pressure;
- repeated relationship dynamics;
- difficulty making or sustaining important decisions;
- a loss of direction during a transition;
- disconnection from the body or personal signals;
- questions arising from meditation or contemplative practice;
- the need for a structured place to think honestly.

This list is not a diagnostic checklist. It describes the territory supportive counselling can often hold.

## What Happens in a Session

Sessions take place by private video call. You will need a stable enough connection, a device with audio and video, and a space where you can speak without being overheard.

The first full consultation is exploratory. We clarify the concern, look at the patterns around it, and decide whether the approach fits. Later sessions become more focused. Conversation may be combined with a simple practice or a concrete experiment to try in daily life.

The work is collaborative. I will offer observations, questions, and challenge where useful, but not a fixed interpretation of who you are or what you should believe.

## Online Privacy and Practical Boundaries

Confidentiality and secure handling of personal information are part of the professional relationship. Before beginning, you should understand how sessions are conducted, what records are kept, how contact happens, and the circumstances in which confidentiality may be limited by safety or legal duties.

Your own setting matters too. Headphones, a private room, device security, and avoiding shared workplace accounts can make online sessions more private.

Online work also has a practical limit in emergencies. I do not provide a crisis response service and may not be physically located near you. If you are at immediate risk, unable to care for yourself, or concerned that you may harm yourself or someone else, use local emergency or clinical services rather than waiting for an online counselling appointment.

## South African Registration, International Delivery

Professional registration and physical location are different questions. My ASCHP registration is South African. I am currently based in India and provide the service online to adults in South Africa and elsewhere.

International clients should understand that professional designations, legal frameworks, and emergency systems vary by country. The fit call is the place to clarify whether remote supportive counselling is appropriate in your circumstances.

## How to Choose Between Wellness and Clinical Care

Begin with the level of need, not with which title sounds more appealing.

A psychologist or psychiatrist is the more appropriate starting point for suspected psychiatric conditions, severe or worsening symptoms, diagnosis, trauma requiring clinical treatment, medication, major impairment in daily functioning, or significant risk.

Wellness counselling may fit when you are functioning but stuck, when the difficulty is developmental or relational, or when you need structured support around change rather than clinical treatment.

If you remain unsure, ask directly. A responsible practitioner should be willing to say when their service is not the right one.
