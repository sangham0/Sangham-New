Open-ended counselling can be valuable, but it can also be difficult to begin when you do not yet know what the work will involve. Sangham's five-session process offers a contained starting point: enough time to understand a pattern, work with it directly, and review what has changed without assuming that counselling must continue indefinitely.

The process is structured, but it is not a script. Each person arrives with a different life, history, and pace. The five stages provide orientation while the actual conversation follows what is present.

## Before the Five Sessions

There are two ways to begin.

The first is a free 15-minute fit call. This is a short conversation to understand what you are looking for, explain the scope of wellness counselling, and see whether working together makes sense. It is not a counselling session and there is no obligation to continue.

The first paid step is an initial 60-minute consultation at R850. This gives us enough time to explore what is happening, experience the approach directly, and decide whether the five-session process is the right next step.

The five-session process costs R3,400 and is paid in advance. Sessions are normally weekly or fortnightly, online by video, and approximately 60 minutes each.

## Session One: Mapping

We begin by building a more accurate picture of the difficulty.

Most people already have a story about what is wrong. That story may contain a great deal of insight, but it may also combine several different problems into one. Mapping slows things down. We look at when the pattern appears, what precedes it, what happens in the body, what you do next, and what the pattern protects or prevents.

The aim is not to collect your entire history. It is to identify the part of the terrain that can be worked with usefully now.

## Session Two: Identifying

The second session moves from the story of the pattern to the mechanisms maintaining it.

Someone may describe procrastination, for example, while the more immediate mechanism is fear of exposure, an unrealistic internal demand, or difficulty tolerating the bodily activation that comes with beginning. A relationship problem may be sustained by a rapid protective response that occurs before either person has time to think.

We try to identify what is actually happening, not what a generic explanation says should be happening.

## Session Three: Intervening

Once the pattern is clearer, we introduce targeted adjustments. Depending on the person, this may include breath and regulation practices, attention training, body-based awareness, a behavioural experiment, a different way of preparing for a difficult conversation, or a more honest boundary.

The intervention should be proportionate and usable. A practice that looks impressive but cannot survive an ordinary Tuesday is not yet useful.

This session is collaborative. I explain why a particular practice or change is being suggested, and we adapt it around your real circumstances.

## Session Four: Applying

The fourth session is where theory meets life. We look at what happened when you tried the adjustment.

What worked? What did not? Where did the old pattern reappear? Did the intervention reach the real difficulty, or reveal that the difficulty lies somewhere else?

This is not a test of whether you followed instructions correctly. Failed experiments often provide the clearest information in the process. Application turns insight into evidence and helps us revise the work around what is actually true.

## Session Five: Consolidating

The final session reviews the whole process. We name what has changed, what remains difficult, which practices or decisions are worth carrying forward, and what support may be useful next.

Sometimes five sessions are enough for the piece of work that was chosen. Sometimes they reveal a deeper process that would benefit from continued counselling. Sometimes the honest conclusion is that a different professional or form of support would be more appropriate.

There is no automatic move into ongoing sessions. The review point is real.

## What Happens Between Sessions

Change rarely occurs only during the hour we speak. Between sessions, you may be asked to observe a pattern, use a brief practice, try a specific action, or record what happens in a recurring situation.

This is not homework for its own sake. The purpose is to bring better information back into the conversation and to test whether insight can become behaviour.

## What the Process Does Not Promise

Five sessions cannot promise resolution, transformation, or a permanent end to a long-standing difficulty. Any service that guarantees that would be claiming too much.

What the structure can offer is a clear first piece of work: a shared map, a more precise understanding of what maintains the problem, practical experiments, and an honest review. For many people, this is enough to create movement where repeated reflection has not.

Wellness counselling is not psychotherapy, psychiatric treatment, diagnosis, or emergency mental health care. Where clinical support is needed, referral is part of responsible practice.
